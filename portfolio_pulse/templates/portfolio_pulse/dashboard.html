Project thesis · one portfolio, three decisions See the shape of the book, decide where to intervene, then understand how the result was made. Read the thesis

Portfolio Pulse gives leaders and customer-success teams one shared account of recurring-revenue health. It begins with a belief: customer condition matters most when it is connected to value, contract timing, and clear evidence. Portfolio Outlook shows the book’s scale, health, concentration, maturity, and renewal exposure. Customer Action Centre turns that picture into six named priorities, explaining why each account requires attention. Revenue Story shows how ARR and customer signals moved, separating current condition from historical performance.

For a CEO, the product answers whether ARR is growing, where revenue is exposed, how concentrated the portfolio is, which industries are weakening, and whether customer signals are deteriorating before financial results. For a CSM, it identifies the next customers to contact, overdue QBRs, approaching renewals, growers and decliners, behavioural warning signs, and gaps in the underlying data.

The ambition is not to predict outcomes with false certainty. It is to create earlier, better conversations using transparent evidence. In future, Portfolio Pulse could learn which interventions work, compare portfolios fairly, identify expansion capacity, replay historical health accurately, and show whether strategic engagement changes renewal outcomes. Its value lies in helping executives ask better questions and helping CSMs act before customer weakness becomes lost ARR.

Every model produces one account-health index from 0 to 100: Critical is 50 or below, Watch is above 50 through 75, and Healthy is above 75. The portfolio headline is the ARR-weighted average, so large accounts influence the book-level result without receiving a better individual score merely because they are large.

{% if selected_health_model.id == "plain_pulse" %}

Plain Pulse · the auditable baseline

Health = clip(100 − deductions + strengths, 0, 100)

Plain Pulse deliberately uses cliffs. It removes 20 points when a QBR is over 180 days old, 20 for overdue payment, 10 below 50% seat utilisation, 10 below 40% active-user rate, 15 for a term of 12 months or less (or 5 for 24 months or less), and 10 when renewal is within six months. It adds small strengths for a recent QBR, utilisation of at least 80%, active-user rate of at least 70%, and one, three, or five years of tenure.

This is the easiest model to reproduce and challenge. Its trade-off is threshold sensitivity: 49% utilisation is penalised while 50% is not. Tickets, segment context, ARR direction, and interactions are intentionally excluded.

{% elif selected_health_model.id == "signal_compass" %}

Signal Compass · gradual rather than cliff-based

Health = clip(100 − gradual deductions × context + strengths, 0, 100)

Signal Compass keeps the transparent 100-point structure but measures degree. QBR age and ticket load ramp between segment-specific clean and poor thresholds. Utilisation gradually loses up to 15 points between 70% and 20%; active-user rate loses up to 10 between 60% and 15%. Payment and short-contract evidence remain explicit deductions.

Context then amplifies the combined deduction: up to 1.25× when renewal is close, 1.15× for a customer younger than six months, and 1.15× when ARR is declining by more than 5%. These factors can compound. This produces finer nuance than Plain Pulse, but the ramps and multipliers are still transparent expert assumptions rather than learned effects.

{% else %}

Retention Horizon · a logistic-shaped experimental index

Health = 100 ÷ (1 + e−z)

The linear value z starts at 0.40. Longevity contributes up to +1.10; QBR recency ranges from +0.70 to −0.70; utilisation from −0.80 to +0.80; active-user rate from −0.70 to +0.70; contract commitment and renewal runway each add or remove up to roughly 0.55 and 0.45. Overdue payment contributes −1.20, while support demand and ARR decline contribute up to −0.45 and −0.55. The logistic curve then converts that total into a bounded 0–100 index.

Here z means the logistic model’s linear predictor; it is not a statistical z-score. The coefficients have not been fitted to churn outcomes, so this is not a renewal probability or trained logistic regression. Its value is showing how a richer weighted model behaves while remaining inspectable.

{% endif %}

Missing QBR: when no QBR is recorded, its effective age becomes customer tenure and data completeness falls. Other missing signals are generally neutral rather than silently imputed.

What this changes: portfolio health, health bands, health-led colours, Sector Weather, and Intervention Queue priority. Historical ARR movement and the Early-Warning Map remain model-independent.

ML boundary: a random forest needs labelled renewal, churn, or contraction outcomes. Clustering can reveal peer archetypes, but clusters do not inherently mean Healthy or Critical. Portfolio Pulse will keep those methods separate until they can add evidence without manufacturing certainty.

The Value Ladder

The ARR-ranked portfolio is divided evenly into Gold, Silver, and Bronze accounts, with each bar showing average ARR. A steep step between tiers signals that a relatively small number of customers carries disproportionate annual value.

The Renewal Horizon

Current ARR is grouped by time remaining on contract, from immediate renewal work to long-dated security. Watch for a heavy red bucket or a large yellow wave that will soon become the team’s active workload.

The Revenue Skyline

The largest 50 customers are ranked by ARR and coloured by health, with account names available on hover. Tall red or amber bars on the left reveal where concentration and customer condition combine into material exposure.

Customer Roots

ARR is grouped by how long customers have remained with the business, moving from young relationships to deeply established ones. A mature green base suggests entrenchment; too much value in pale early-tenure bands means the book has limited shared history.

Sector Weather

Each industry bar shows its ARR split across Critical, Watch, and Healthy accounts. Look for large sectors with weak colour mixes or a high single-customer share, because apparent diversification may conceal concentrated exposure.

How eligibility, stability, materiality, and validation work

EligibilityA full signal needs at least 10 monthly observations, 8 usage observations, and a 12-month span. Short histories remain visible but are labelled early.

StabilityARR and usage compare the median of the first three observations with the median of the final three, reducing single-month noise.

MaterialityRed means ARR ≥ −2% with usage ≤ −10 points; ARR ≥ +20% with usage ≤ +2 points; or ARR ≤ −10% with usage ≤ −5 points. Green requires ARR ≥ +10% and usage ≥ +10 points.

ValidationThese are transparent evidence rules, not churn probabilities. Outcome validation remains pending until labelled renewal and contraction history exists.
